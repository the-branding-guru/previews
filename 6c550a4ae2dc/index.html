section 01 · The shape of it
Thirty seconds — the whole thing in your head

One child. Three doors into the same record.

That is the entire architecture. A child does the work. The work becomes a record. Three different people look at that same record through three different doors, and each door shows only what that person needs and can only do what that person should.

Door Who walks through it What they can do
Hero Portal The student Do the work. Log it. Reflect. Own it.
Empowerment Coach Staff See everyone. Spot who is slipping. Note and recommend.
Parent Portal The family See growth. Practise at home. Cannot edit anything.
◆ If you only remember one sentence

"A child does the work, and the same record shows up three ways — as quests to the kid, as a cohort to the coach, as growth to the parent. Nobody re-types anything, and nobody sees a number the child hasn't seen first."

Two systems sit underneath and make the numbers mean something. S.O.S. — Social Operating Skills is yours: how a young person operates between people. O.M.G. — Open Minded Goals is also yours: how they think inside their own head, Pre-K through 12th. Everything the portals measure comes from these.

section 07 · Your two systems
If they ask what's underneath — two minutes

The part nobody else has.

Anyone can build a dashboard. The reason these numbers mean anything is that they measure two systems you built. If the conversation goes deep, this is where you take it.

System In one breath The detail if pressed
S.O.S.
Social Operating Skills		 How a young person operates between people. Four parts — read the room, read yourself, choose your response, stay authentic. Five levels from Unaware to Leadership. Eighteen named skills. Kids score themselves after activities.
O.M.G.
Open Minded Goals		 How they operate inside their own head. Pre-K through 12th grade. Fourteen levels, two decks each — Cognitive Thinking and Rational Thinking. Complete and ready. David already greenlit this at his house.
◆ Why the pair matters — worth saying out loud

"One without the other gives you a kid who's socially smooth and internally a mess, or internally sorted and socially invisible. You'd be running both."

The five S.O.S. levels are the invention. A child is never "bad at people" — they're at a 2 today and they can name what a 3 looks like. No school can say that sentence without this scale.
