section 05 · S.O.S.
Part two — S.O.S. · Social Operating Skills · already in the curriculum

Most children are taught "be yourself." S.O.S. teaches them to read the room.

S.O.S. — Social Operating Skills — is the signature system. Jack Polo's, built by him, and already written into the Wonderstorm curriculum with its own page on the academy site. It is not a worksheet or a unit; it is a complete operating system for how a young person moves through other people: a thesis, four teachable parts, a five-level scale, eighteen named skills, and the activities that practise them. Its core instrument is Social Calibration.

The name carries the point. A child who cannot read a room is sending one — and S.O.S. is what answers it. The thesis it opens with is the reason it exists. "Be yourself" is the standard advice and it is useless in the moment a child is misreading a room — because it tells them the problem is who they are rather than what they are noticing. S.O.S. replaces identity advice with a skill: adjust the delivery, never the person.

The four parts of Social Calibration The question the child learns to ask
01 · Reading the Room Can I notice the emotions, the energy, who is engaged, who feels left out, when tension is rising, when humour lands? Observers before speakers.
02 · Reading Yourself Can I recognise my own emotion, tone, body language, how much space I am taking up, how my actions affect others? Leadership begins with self-awareness.
03 · Choosing an Intentional Response What is needed here? What outcome do I want? What would a great leader do? This is where emotional regulation becomes leadership.
04 · Staying Authentic Am I adjusting my delivery without abandoning my identity? Like the volume on a speaker — the music stays the same, the volume changes with the room.
Level What the hero says about themselves
1 · Unaware "I didn’t notice anyone else."
2 · Noticing "I noticed others, but reacted automatically."
3 · Adjusting "I recognised what was happening and made a better choice."
4 · Intentional "I helped improve the group dynamic."
5 · Leadership "I noticed what others needed before they did, and positively influenced the culture."
◆ Why the five levels are the whole invention

Heroes evaluate themselves after activities against this scale — and it shifts the conversation from judgement to growth. A child is never "bad at people." They are at a 2 today and they can name what a 3 looks like. There is no version of that sentence available to a school without this scale.

Eighteen skills are practised by name: active listening · eye contact · voice volume · reading facial expressions · personal space · body language · asking questions · timing · humour · conflict resolution · giving feedback · receiving feedback · welcoming new people · including others · navigating disagreements · public speaking · group conversations · one-on-one conversations.

Where it lives: the academy site → Social Calibration in the main menu (added to the top-level navigation today — it had been reachable only from one button partway down the home page), and the coach’s 360 & Calibration tab, where the whole cohort’s distribution across the five levels is visible at once. The self-evaluations feed the Social Calibration figure the parent sees. One system, three surfaces.

section 06 · The other instruments
Part two — what the tracker is actually tracking

A tracker measures something. These are the somethings.

S.O.S. is the spine. These are the instruments that work alongside it — also Jack Polo's, also already wired into the portals. The dashboard is the easy half; any competent team can draw a progress ring. The hard half is having something worth putting inside it, precise enough that a percentage actually means a child changed.

Instrument How it works Why it is necessary
Social Calibration (the core of S.O.S.) Four parts — Reading the Room · Reading Yourself · Choosing an Intentional Response · Staying Authentic — scored on five levels from Unaware to Leadership, across eighteen named skills (active listening, timing, humour, conflict resolution, including others, receiving feedback…). "Good with people" is not teachable. This breaks it into eighteen things a child can practise on a Tuesday and a coach can actually observe. The five levels give the same child a next rung instead of a verdict.
I Master Me A named state-shift protocol: Frustrated → Balanced, Overwhelmed → Centered, Anxious → Grounded, Angry → Composed — each with the specific move that gets there (balloon breathing, name-it-then-five-breaths, feet on the floor and three things you can see, put the thought on trial). Self-regulation is the gate on every other skill. A dysregulated child cannot access a single thing the school teaches. This gives them a tool with a name they can reach for alone, and it is logged — so regulation becomes visible progress rather than invisible effort.
The 3 B's Brain, Buddy, Book. What do I already know? Who around me can help? Where is this written down? It replaces "ask the teacher" as the reflex. Three seconds of self-reliance before dependence — and it is the answer key to the resource question in every check-in.
The 10/10 Rule The hero rates their own work, then states what a 10 would have looked like and what would move it there. This is self-assessment with teeth. It trains honest calibration — a child who consistently scores their own 6 as a 9 is a coaching signal you would otherwise never see.
The Check-In Ten standing questions per session — proudest moment, the struggle, why it was a struggle, what resources would help, what changes next time, where they feel most challenged, who they guided and who guided them. It converts a session into evidence. Same ten questions every time is what makes eight weeks of them comparable — that is where the trend lines on the Coach dashboard come from.
360° Feedback Five axes — Taker↔Giver, Tough-minded↔Warm-hearted, Disagreeable↔Agreeable, Talks-over↔Active-listener, Waits↔Takes-initiative — from peers and coaches. Character is the one thing a child cannot self-report accurately. The 360 is the only place the group's read enters the record.
S.M.A.R.T. Goals + the Chronicle Goals broken into steps the hero ticks off, each completion writing a line into their running story. It closes the loop between intention and evidence. The chronicle is what a child reads back at the end of a year — the proof they became someone.

Every one of these is already wired into the portals you just walked. The XP values, the mastery percentage, the eight-week trend lines and the Needs Support flag are all computed from these instruments. That is why the tracker is not a generic dashboard — it can only measure what it was built to measure.

section 07 · O.M.G.
Open Minded Goals — the curriculum David already greenlit · now BUILT into the Leader portal

Fourteen levels. Pre-K through twelfth grade.

O.M.G. — Open Minded Goals is a cognitive-thinking and rational-behaviour curriculum spanning Pre-K to 12th grade. It is not a supplement bolted onto a subject; it is a parallel spine that runs alongside standard curriculum, teaching a child how to think about what they are being taught.

◆ Built, not planned (Sep 4 2026)

O.M.G. is live in the Leader portal as the fifth programme: all 1,400 cards, fourteen levels, both decks. A leader draws a daily hand of five at their level — Cognitive cards are done and checked in, Rational cards are thought through in three lines and rated — and every hand writes into Today's Plan and the Coach view. The remaining decision is the slot in the week, not whether.

Dimension What exists today
Span 14 levels — Pre-K, Kindergarten, and grades 1 through 12
Structure Two decks per level — Cognitive Thinking and Rational Thinking
State The card deck is complete and extracted, verified against source
Lineage Built from Jack Polo's own CBT library — no third-party licence sits underneath it
◆ Why this pairs with the social-skills instruments rather than duplicating them

S.O.S. governs how a child operates between people. O.M.G. governs how they operate inside their own head — the thought before the behaviour. One without the other produces a child who is either socially fluent and internally chaotic, or internally ordered and socially invisible. Wonderstorm would be running both.

For the record, and worth stating plainly in the room: David greenlit adding O.M.G. to the Wonderstorm curriculum in a prior conversation at his home. This section is not a new pitch — it is the specification of something already agreed, so it can now be scheduled rather than re-decided.

Each now has an original Wonderstorm replacement, running in the Leader Portal today, that does the same job and writes its scores straight into the Coach and Parent views. Content deepens every week; the engines are live.

Phase What happens Why this order
1 · Own the tracking layer One daily plan and one progress view across every subject, whatever the content source. This is the pain you described. Immediate, and it removes the separate-login problem before a single lesson is rebuilt.
2 · Replace the lightest content Video-plus-task lessons in Wonderstorm's own footage and voice — the Generation Genius job first. A provable win at low risk, run side by side with the subscription for one cycle before it is cancelled.
3 · Commission the heavy curricula Literacy and the maths sequence — authored, levelled, original. Lexia and Teaching Textbooks are years of content. That is a funded programme with an author and a timeline, not a sprint, and we will not sell it as one.
◆ What changes when a rented tool becomes an owned one

A subscription is a cost that recurs forever and leaves nothing behind. An owned instrument is an asset: it stops costing, it carries your name, it can be changed the week a coach asks for it, its output feeds the tracker instead of sitting in a separate login — and it becomes something Wonderstorm could one day licence to another school rather than pay another school's vendor.

One boundary, stated up front so it never becomes a question later: we rebuild the job, never the other vendor's actual work. Nothing is copied, cloned, or lifted. Each replacement is original — that is what makes it Wonderstorm's to own, and what keeps it defensible.
